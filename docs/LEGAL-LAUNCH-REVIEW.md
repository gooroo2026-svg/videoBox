# VideoBox legal and safety launch review

This is a product-readiness checklist, not legal advice. Rules depend on where the operator is based, where users are located, and the ages and features VideoBox supports. Final terms and privacy language should be reviewed for those locations before public launch.

## Current app audit

- The Privacy, Terms, and Content Guidelines pages exist, but they are explicitly drafts and still contain owner/contact and process placeholders.
- The app stores account details, uploaded video files and descriptions, saved videos, likes, comments, follows, reports, and view counts. The Privacy page should accurately describe each item, Supabase, any future advertising providers, retention, and how a person can exercise available rights.
- The earlier setup policy allowed every signed-in user to read raw `video_likes` and `creator_follows` rows, including other users' internal IDs. The updated setup script restricts those rows to their owner/admin and adds aggregate-count RPCs for the UI. This only takes effect after the SQL is applied, and any other permissive policies in the deployed database must also be checked.
- The setup now grants regular accounts access only to needed comment columns, excluding the commenter's internal `user_id`; the watch page no longer selects that ID. This privacy restriction also depends on applying the updated SQL and checking for any other permissive grants or policies in Supabase.
- Creator Studio now uploads new drafts to a private `video-drafts` bucket; the admin review page issues temporary preview URLs and copies approved files into the public `videos` bucket. This depends on applying the updated `supabase/core-feature-setup.sql` and has not been tested against the live project. Older drafts uploaded to the public bucket remain exposed by direct URL until migrated or removed. Supabase confirms public-bucket files can be fetched by anyone who has the URL. Do not claim old drafts are private. See Supabase's [bucket access model](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/storage/buckets/fundamentals).
- Video and comment reporting now collect a concern category, details, and the signed-in reporter's email; admins can hide a reported published video or comment and mark the report reviewed. There is still no end-user appeal workflow. The report form is a moderation intake, not a complete jurisdiction-specific copyright notice/counter-notice process. For an existing database, run `supabase/legal-readiness-update.sql` once to add the report-contact/comment columns and update its insert policy; the complete setup script also includes these changes.
- Admins can suspend accounts. There is no self-service account/data deletion workflow. Until one exists, publish a real support contact and establish a reliable request-and-response process before accepting public accounts.
- A Supabase schema screenshot shared during development showed a `users` table with a `password_hash` column, while the current app source uses Supabase Auth and contains no references to that table or column. Its contents, RLS, and purpose cannot be verified from this repository. Inspect it in the live Supabase project, including its relationship to `videos.user_id`; remove any unused credential field/table or strictly restrict it, and never store user passwords outside Supabase Auth. Treat any real credential exposure as a security incident.
- AdSense code is optional; the script and ad slots now fail closed unless both `NEXT_PUBLIC_ADSENSE_CLIENT` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_ADSENSE_CONSENT_READY=true` are set. The app still has no consent-management interface or age-treatment logic, so keep that readiness flag false until a suitable consent-management provider is installed and tested and age-based ad treatment is designed for the selected countries. The flag is only a deployment switch; it does not itself provide consent. Google's publisher rules prohibit personalized ads based on activity of known under-13 users or child-directed areas; Google also has age-treatment settings and teen ad protections. Google requires the AdSense account holder to be 18 or older; if the owner is younger, Google says a parent or guardian must hold the account and receive payments.
- Signup asks new users for a birth date before account details and blocks dates under 18 in the browser. Existing signed-in accounts without current flags are also directed through the age and policy-acceptance screens. Neither flow saves the birth date; the app stores an `18_plus_self_attested` flag and a policy-version label in user-editable metadata. The linked Terms/Privacy checkbox is a useful clickwrap prompt, but this metadata is not a durable acceptance log; users can change it, and direct auth requests can bypass the browser. Add verified age assurance and a server-controlled policy acceptance record before treating these as launch controls.
- Children are deferred to a separate later release. Before that release, address public user video storage, public comments, parent account/control path and consent verification, and child-specific moderation/appeal safeguards; have the child-safety design reviewed.
- Creator payouts, subscriptions, and withdrawals are not active. Do not describe them as available or accept money for them until a payment provider and complete payment terms are ready.

## Decisions the owner must supply

- Operator's country and legal/business name, and the countries the service will accept at first launch. **Current answer:** operator is based in Nigeria and intends to allow users worldwide. This needs country-by-country review; a generic policy cannot establish compliance everywhere.
- Minimum permitted age; whether anyone under 18 may create an account or upload content. **Current answer:** phase one is adults-only (18+); children are deferred until appropriate safeguards are ready. The current self-attestation screen is not verified age assurance or tamper-proof enforcement. A global minimum age still needs country-by-country review.
- A support and privacy contact that will be monitored.
- Whether VideoBox will accept only original/licensed work, and how copyright complaints, takedowns, counter-notices, and appeals will be received and handled.
- Whether ads will be enabled at launch and whether visitors from the EEA, UK, or Switzerland will be accepted.
- Whether paid plans or creator earnings will be introduced at launch (currently no).

## Required before a public launch

1. Replace every draft notice with complete, consistent policies and remove all placeholder text. Publish the operator/contact details, effective date, and policy-change process.
2. Complete Terms covering account eligibility, acceptable use, user-content ownership and display license, moderation, suspension, appeals, termination, disclaimers, disputes, and governing law as appropriate for the launch locations.
3. Complete the Privacy Notice with actual data collected, purposes, service providers, advertising/cookies, retention, security, international transfers, user choices/rights, and contact/request instructions.
4. Complete Content Guidelines and working routes for reporting harmful or infringing content, including individual comments if they remain available. Define how staff/admins review a report, restrict or remove content, preserve necessary records, notify affected users, and accept appeals.
5. Add an operational account/data request process, including deletion requests and what records must or may be retained. Test it with the real support contact.
6. For phase one, strengthen the 18+ eligibility rule across signup and existing accounts, with country-specific age limits where needed. The current self-attestation is not a strong enforcement control. Before any later under-18/under-13 phase, implement an age flow before collecting account details, a legally appropriate verifiable parent/guardian consent process, parent access/deletion controls, and child-appropriate privacy defaults. A checkbox or ordinary email-confirmation link is not a substitute for verifiable parental consent.
7. If AdSense is enabled, publish its required cookie/data disclosures and implement the consent experience required for the regions served. Google's publisher rules require a privacy disclosure for advertising cookies; Google requires a certified CMP integrated with the IAB TCF for personalized ads shown to users in the EEA, UK, or Switzerland. Before any child or teen audience is admitted, configure age treatment and block prohibited personalized targeting before loading the ad script; test the behavior by age/region.
8. If payment, subscriptions, or creator payouts are enabled later, add separate clear terms for price, billing, cancellations/refunds, payout eligibility, calculation, timing, fees, tax responsibilities, disputes, and account closure before taking payment or promising earnings.
9. Have a qualified lawyer or local small-business/legal clinic review the final documents and operation for the selected countries. No policy set can promise that nobody will sue or that every dispute will be avoided.

## Nigeria and worldwide availability

- Review the Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023 and NDPC guidance for the actual service. In particular, confirm whether VideoBox meets a category or threshold for a data controller/processor of major importance and whether registration or other compliance steps apply; do not assume either way without checking the current NDPC rules.
- NDPC's published registration guidance explicitly lists public social-media app developers and proprietors in the Ultra-High Level (UHL) category. VideoBox's public creator uploads, follows, comments, and video sharing resemble that category; treat UHL registration as a priority to confirm before public launch rather than assuming the two test accounts exempt the operator. NDPC's FAQ limits its under-200 Compliance Audit Return exception to Ordinary-High Level (OHL) entities, and says those entities still renew registration annually. Confirm VideoBox's classification, current amended guidance, current registration/CAR fees and timing, and any DPO/DPIA requirements with NDPC or a licensed DPCO. The prepared inquiry is in `docs/NDPC-CLASSIFICATION-QUESTIONS-DRAFT.md`. [Official registration guidance](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Updated-Guidance-Notice-on-Registtration-2024.pdf), [GAID 2025](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf), [NDPC FAQ](https://ndpc.gov.ng/faqs/), and [NDPC 2025 Annual Report noting amended registration guidance](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Print_NDPC-Annual-Report-2025-1.pdf).
- Identify the Supabase project region and every processor/subprocessor handling account, video, report, and analytics data. Because the operator is in Nigeria and access is intended worldwide, have a qualified reviewer assess Nigeria's cross-border transfer mechanism and the rules in each enabled country; update the privacy notice to explain the actual transfer locations and safeguards. NDPC's FAQ summarizes the transfer framework and possible mechanisms: https://ndpc.gov.ng/faqs/.
- The Act has a specific provision for personal data of children and people lacking capacity, including appropriate age/consent verification mechanisms. VideoBox now has a browser-only 18+ self-declaration, but it is bypassable and does not verify age; it does not satisfy a completed age-control review by itself.
- The immediate phase is adults-first; children are deferred to a separate later release. New and existing accounts now see an 18+ date check and self-attestation, but the flow is not verified age enforcement. Supabase user metadata can be changed by the account owner, so a server-side check of that metadata should not be treated as tamper-proof. Before public access, validate and strengthen the 18+ rule for each launch country. Before any later under-18/under-13 release, determine and implement the lawful parental notice/consent, age assurance, and parent access/deletion path for each relevant country. A parent's email link alone may not be sufficient verification for every applicable rule. The exact path needs jurisdiction-specific legal review and a chosen implementation/provider.
- In the U.S., COPPA can apply to services directed to children under 13 or with actual knowledge they collect children's personal information. Covered services must generally give parents direct notice and obtain verifiable parental consent before collecting, using, or disclosing children's personal information, and provide parental access/deletion controls.
- In the EU, the age for a child to consent to certain online data processing varies by member state from 13 to 16; below the applicable age, parental authorisation is required where that consent basis applies. A global service needs a country-aware approach rather than one universal age statement.
- UK: an adults-only rule does not automatically remove child-design duties. The ICO says its Children’s Code can cover online services likely to be accessed by under-18s, including services not aimed at children; it expects a documented risk assessment and protections such as high-privacy defaults and data minimisation. VideoBox needs a country-specific scope assessment before accepting UK users, especially before any later child release.
- EU/EEA: GDPR Article 8's 13–16 age range applies specifically when consent is the legal basis for directly offered information-society services; it does not settle VideoBox's full lawful-basis analysis. The Digital Services Act may also apply to the user-upload platform depending on VideoBox's provider role and size. The current report form is a useful start but has not been assessed against formal notice/action, decision-reasons, and complaint requirements.
- Worldwide availability means checking additional countries' privacy, consumer, child-safety, copyright, and online-service rules. The app cannot promise worldwide compliance just by publishing one global Terms page. Get qualified advice on the initial scope and staged expansion.
